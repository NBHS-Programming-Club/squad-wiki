The shared ladder

From “what is a variable?” to university-level work.

Every pathway uses the same progression. Your projects and technical details change; your learning habits get stronger.

LEVEL 01

Starter

Computer literacy, tools, variables, conditions, loops, functions, and debugging.

LEVEL 02

Builder

Core pathway concepts, small projects, version control, testing, and clear documentation.

LEVEL 03

Competitor

Harder constraints, larger systems, performance, collaboration, and public showcases.

LEVEL 04

Researcher

University theory, papers, advanced projects, independent questions, and teaching others.

Each roadmap is intentionally distinct. Choose one and use its milestones to plan your next project.

Python pathway

Teach machines to learn from data.

Begin with readable Python and end by understanding model design, evaluation, and modern AI research.

PythonNumPyPyTorchLinear algebra
01 · FoundationsPython syntax, functions, files, modules, Git, testing, and computational thinking.
02 · Data & mathsNumPy, pandas, visualisation, probability, statistics, vectors, matrices, and derivatives.
03 · Machine learningRegression, classification, trees, clustering, feature engineering, validation, and ethics.
04 · Deep learningNeural networks, backpropagation, CNNs, transformers, embeddings, and experiment tracking.
05 · Advanced studyOptimisation, generative models, reinforcement learning, distributed training, and reading papers.
Project finish lineBuild a reproducible model with a dataset card, evaluation report, and honest limitations.
Godot pathway

Turn ideas into playable worlds.

Learn the craft of games: systems, feel, art pipelines, player feedback, and finishing small things.

GodotGDScript2D / 3DGame design
01 · FoundationsEditor, scenes, nodes, scripts, input, variables, functions, and a tiny 2D game.
02 · Game systemsPhysics, collisions, UI, animation, audio, cameras, state machines, and save data.
03 · Design & polishLevel design, difficulty curves, juice, accessibility, playtesting, and iteration.
04 · 3D & architectureLighting, materials, navigation, shaders, procedural generation, and reusable systems.
05 · Advanced studyRendering pipelines, networking, ECS ideas, AI agents, optimisation, and technical design.
Project finish lineRelease a polished game with a build, controls guide, credits, and a postmortem.
C++ pathway

Turn constraints into elegant solutions.

Train for informatics competitions while learning the algorithms that power computer science.

C++AlgorithmsGraphsContests
01 · FoundationsC++ syntax, types, loops, functions, arrays, strings, complexity, and contest workflow.
02 · Core structuresSorting, binary search, prefix sums, stacks, queues, maps, sets, and two pointers.
03 · Graphs & DPBFS, DFS, shortest paths, spanning trees, dynamic programming, greedy, and backtracking.
04 · Advanced algorithmsFlow, matching, segment trees, Fenwick trees, string algorithms, geometry, and number theory.
05 · University theoryProofs, amortised analysis, NP-completeness, computational models, and research problems.
Project finish lineCompete regularly, explain editorials, and create a problem set with tested solutions.

Squad handbook

Questions new members ask.

The rules are simple: be kind, stay consistent, and make your reasoning visible.

Can I switch pathways later?

Choose one pathway for a full learning cycle so you can build depth. A switch is possible after a cycle if your interests change; talk to a mentor before moving.

Do I need to be good at maths?

No. Start where you are. Maths becomes useful at different points: graphs and logic in informatics, algebra and statistics in ML, and geometry or physics in games.

What counts as progress?

A working project, a solved problem, a clear explanation, or a useful question. Track what you can now do without copying a solution.

How do competitions fit in?

Competitions are practice with a clock, not the only measure of skill. Informatics students compete most directly; every pathway can enter showcases, jams, hackathons, or challenges.
